Where it started

The design and content you approved looked great, but underneath it was held together by layers of patches. The homepage loaded 7 to 9 MB of images on first view, spacing and text sizes varied from section to section, some text and buttons were too small on phones, the motion felt abrupt, and neither form could actually send anything.

What I did

  • Rebuilt the foundation. The site now runs on a modern, standard setup. I moved everything over with no visible change first, then refined from there. It's faster, more reliable and much easier to update safely.

  • Much faster. Images are now sized for each screen, so the first view dropped from 7–9 MB to about 0.5 MB on phones and 1 MB on desktop, roughly 90% lighter.

  • Refined design and motion. Details in the next section.

  • Forms ready to switch on. The careers application and the security request form are both built and tested. They can't send yet: we need the email setup and a few answers from you (next message).

  • Security and launch prep. I reviewed the site's security and fixed what I found. I also went through all 69 pages on the current WordPress site and set up redirects so old links keep working.

What you'll notice visually

At a glance

Most of the work shows on every screen size. The examples below cover desktop and phone, with a few of the clearest changes on each. They're a sample, not the full list.

Across the whole site

Consistent layout. Edges, spacing and heading sizes used to vary from section to section (seven slightly different heading sizes). Now everything follows one rhythm, so the site feels more polished and intentional.

One color palette. Popups, the blog and the handbook still had older greens from previous updates made mixed in. Everything now uses the approved olive.

Readable text. Some labels were as small as 7–10px. Nothing is smaller than 12px now, which is easier to read, especially on phones.

Cleaner icons. Icons were a mix of text symbols and hand-drawn shapes. They're now one matching set, and arrows are consistent: → stays on the site, ↗ opens another site.

Easier tapping on phones. Many buttons were too small to tap reliably. They now have a comfortable tap area without looking any bigger.

Page by page

Header and mobile menu. One header is now shared by every page. On phones the menu opens as a clean full-screen panel with clear rows and a call button.

Top of the homepage. The headline drifted out of line with the header, more so on wide screens. It now lines up with the logo, and the map lines up with the Request button, as in the approved layout. The truck "Lights" button was tiny and covered the bumper on phones; it's now readable and out of the way.

Map. On phones the town names were cut off and markers piled on top of each other. Names are now readable and markers stay distinct at every zoom.

The map also had the biggest rework of any part of the site, on desktop and on phones:

  • One search instead of three controls. There was a search box, a separate "Jump to a destination" list and a "Full valley" button. Now one field does all three: click or tap it to see every destination, grouped into headquarters, Valley communities, and clients and event venues, or type to narrow the list. Matching letters are highlighted, and the reset button sits on the map with the zoom buttons.

  • Results that fit the screen. Search results used to drop over the filters and the map, and on phones they were cut off at the bottom of the card. On desktop the list now opens under the field, the same width as the field. On phones it opens as a panel from the bottom of the screen, with its own search box, room for the full list, and a handle to pull it away.

  • Easier on phones. The filters sit in one row you can swipe, and the map's buttons and filters have a full-size tap area.

  • Smoother and quicker. Explore the Map now grows the card into the full map and shrinks it back on close, instead of cutting to a popup. The map also starts as soon as the page is ready instead of waiting for every image to load.

Why this is better

  • Familiar. It works the way Google Maps and Apple Maps do: one search box that suggests places as you type, and on phones a panel that slides up from the bottom. Visitors have nothing new to learn.
  • Thumb-friendly. A bottom panel keeps results within reach of the thumb and leaves the map in view behind it. The map's controls have tap areas of at least 44 px, Apple's recommended minimum.
  • Accessible. The search follows the W3C's standard pattern for this kind of field, so it works fully from a keyboard (arrow keys, Enter, Escape) and screen readers read out each option.
  • Nothing hidden. Results no longer cover the controls, and a marker never covers a town name.

Service and blog cards. On phones, service descriptions spilled past the card edges; that's fixed. On hover, the photo now zooms gently inside the card instead of the whole card jumping.

Company timeline. The borders looked broken. It's now one clean line, and opening a year lights up that step.

Client carousel. The arrows sometimes skipped or got stuck. It now moves one client at a time, and the edges fade to show there are more clients.

Popups and forms. On phones the close button covered content while scrolling, and parts of the request form overlapped. Both are cleaned up.

Animations and transitions

Popups used to appear and vanish abruptly. They now open and close smoothly.

Scrolling. Sections ease in as you scroll, at the gradual pace you asked for. The family section used to slide in from two directions; it now rises in as one piece.

New touches. Testimonials cross-fade, the map grows smoothly into its full view, the mobile menu slides in, and opening an article from the blog transitions instead of cutting.

Feels responsive. Buttons react when pressed, and hover effects no longer get stuck on after a tap on phones.

Considerate. Animations pause when they're off screen, which saves battery. For visitors who turn off motion on their device, the "Resume" buttons used to do nothing; they now work.

The content, artwork, client locations and testimonials are unchanged, and the employee handbook is still unlisted and hidden from search.
