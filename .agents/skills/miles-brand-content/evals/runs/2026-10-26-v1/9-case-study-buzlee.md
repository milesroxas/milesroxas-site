# Local events without the noise

## Ranked, rated and sponsored

In a small town, a local business's event usually ends up on a platform where ads, reviews and ranking algorithms decide what deserves attention. Buzlee is a digital bulletin board for small towns: one feed and one map for what is on this week, posted by local businesses and approved by a person.

## The bulletin board already worked

The model for Buzlee already existed off the screen. A business pins a flyer to a bulletin board, anyone walking past can read it, and nothing decides that one flyer deserves more attention than another. Buzlee keeps those rules and puts the board on a phone.

A small town also has few enough events each week for a person to read every post before it goes up. That makes human approval workable here, where a larger platform would hand the job to software. ## Decisions and what they cost

The founder and I shaped the MVP around three decisions, and each one gave something up. [check: which of these were the founder's calls and which were Miles's]

- **A person approves every post.** A flyer goes live only after someone reads it, rather than when an automated filter passes it. The cost is time: someone reviews every post, and a business waits before its event appears. [check: who approves posts and how long approval usually takes]
- **Nothing is ranked.** The feed and the map show what is on, and no algorithm pushes an event up for being popular. The cost is that Buzlee never learns what a person likes and shows them more of it.
- **No ads and no ratings.** A business can't pay to rise, and no event carries a score. That gives up advertising as a way to pay for the app.

## What I would keep

Most of Buzlee's design is in what it refuses: ads, ratings and ranking. I would decide those refusals first again, before any feature. What I haven't settled is scale: a person can read every flyer in one small town, and I don't know yet whether that holds as Buzlee adds towns.
