How to use the review tool

This tool is where you read what the miles-brand-content skill writes, judge it in your own words, and decide what to change: the skill, or the benchmark that grades it. The benchmark scores every output; your reading decides what the scores are worth.

  1. Judge blind. Rate an output before you see any score.
  2. Compare. Put two outputs side by side and pick the better one.
  3. Read Insights. See where you and the bench agree, and where you don't.
  4. Tweak. Change the skill, a single piece, or the benchmark.
  5. Generate and repeat. Write new outputs, with any model, and judge again.

Start it

From the repo:

pnpm content-review

Then open localhost:4330. The tool reads .env for three keys, and works without any of them for reading and judging:

NeedsFor
TYPESAFE_API_KEYGrading new outputs with Jev
OPENAI_API_KEYWriting with OpenAI models

The screen

  1. Outputs. Grouped by test case. Each row is one output: the skill version that wrote it on the first line ("v2 · Claims need a source"), then the model, and the sample number when a run wrote more than one. The chip is the bench total.
  2. Scores. The six layers the bench grades, with B's difference from A in green or red.
  3. The paragraph margin. How specific (green bar) and how promotional (orange bar) Jev found each paragraph, plus badges for a decision (D), a cost or setback (S), and how AI work is credited (AI).
  4. Claims. A wavy amber underline is a claim the evidence does not hold. Red is a claim the evidence contradicts. Hover to see the nearest evidence.
  5. Panel tabs. Judge, Insights, Knobs, Skill diff and Generate.
  6. Blind. Hides every score while you judge.

1. Judge blind

  1. Turn on Blind (or press b). Scores, model names and Jev's notes disappear, so the numbers don't steer your reading.
  2. Rate the whole piece from 1 to 5 (keys 1 to 5).
  3. Mark each layer Good or Bad in plain terms: the opening and ending, whether it is specific and honest, whether it sounds like you, whether the facts hold, whether it reads well.
  4. Write notes. To note one paragraph, click its left margin.

Everything saves as you go, to evals/reviews.json. The case's expectations are listed under the notes: tick the ones the output meets.

2. Compare and read the annotations

Click an output to open it as A. ⌥-click another to open it as B: an older skill version, another model, another sample, or a rewrite. Then turn Blind off.

  1. Layer scores. Hover one for what produced it, such as "7 of 8 claims supported".
  2. Flags. The specific problems the bench found. A contradicted claim always opens this list.
  3. Meters. Specific (green) and selling (orange), per paragraph.
  4. Badges. D: a decision against an alternative. S: a cost, failure or open question. AI: AI did part of the work, green when the split between AI and you is stated.
  5. Underlines. Claims not in the evidence (amber) or contradicted (red).
  6. Check markers. Gaps the writer flagged for you to confirm. These are good: the skill asked instead of inventing.

Pick A, B or Tie in the Judge panel and say why. The panel then tells you whether the bench agreed.

3. Read Insights

  1. The grid. For each layer, how your Good and Bad marks line up with the bench's score. A bench score of 70 or more counts as good (change it in Knobs → agree at).
  2. The verdicts. Where to act. The four cells mean different things:
  3. Your rating against the total. Each dot is an output. A rank correlation above 0.6 means the bench orders outputs the way you do.
CellWhat it meansWhat to change
Both goodYou and the bench agree it worksNothing
Both badYou and the bench agree it falls shortThe skill. The verdict names the section of articles.md or SKILL.md.
Bench missedYou marked it bad; the bench scored it highThe benchmark. A Jev question is too lenient, or a weight too low.
Too harshYou marked it good; the bench scored it lowThe benchmark. A question is too strict, or the evidence pack is missing a fact.

Click any count to list those outputs and open one.

4. Tweak

The benchmark: Knobs

Every slider rescores every output instantly, from the answers Jev already gave: no new calls, no cost. Watch the line at the top, Agreement with your marks: you want it to go up. Save writes evals/bench-config.json, which bench.mjs reads from then on. A wrong reading that no weight can fix is a question to reword in scripts/bench.mjs; then use Grade again in Generate.

The skill: Skill diff

Every run keeps a copy of the skill that wrote it, so you can compare any two versions, or the last version against your unsaved edits ("Working tree"). Edit the skill in your editor, or ask Claude to apply your notes from evals/reviews.json. The tool notices the files changed and asks you to name the new version when you generate.

5. Generate: new outputs, rewrites, other models

  1. Skill version. If the skill files changed since the last version, name the new one in a few words ("Shorter openings") and say what changed. If nothing changed, outputs join the current version.
  2. What to write. A test case (optionally with your own prompt), a new brief with its evidence, or a rewrite of the output you have open, with your direction.
  3. Writers. Pick an engine, a model and an effort. Add up to four writers to compare models and effort on the same request; each gets its own run.
EngineHow it writesEffortCost shown
Claude · Claude CodeA full agent session that loads the skill, reads its references and runs the lint, the way you use it every daylow, medium, high, xhigh, maxDollars per sample
OpenAI · APIOne call with the skill and its references in the prompt, then one repair call with the lint's findings. Cheap and controllednone, minimal, low, medium, high, xhigh, max (reasoning models only)Tokens per sample
OpenAI · Codex CLIA full agent session, like Claude Code. Shows up once the Codex CLI is installed and logged inminimal to xhighNone

For a fair test of a model or an effort, change one thing within one engine. The API engine and the agent engines differ in more than the model, so a gap between them is partly the harness. Each new output is graded automatically and appears in the rail with its version and model.

6. Keep what you learn

Make it an anchor… in the Judge panel copies an output into evals/anchors as a good or bad example. A bad anchor lists the layers you marked bad, and the benchmark's self-test then checks that the bench always scores it below the good one:

node --env-file=.env .agents/skills/miles-brand-content/scripts/bench.mjs --self-test

Every rewrite you make by hand is the best data there is. Save your version as a good anchor and the original as a bad one, and the grader learns your taste.

When something is off

Port 4330 is takenlsof -nP -iTCP:4330 -sTCP:LISTEN shows what holds it; or start with REVIEW_PORT=4340 pnpm content-review
New outputs have no scoresTYPESAFE_API_KEY is missing from .env. Add it, restart, then Generate → Grade again
An engine says "not set up"Its line under Writers says what it needs
An OpenAI model refuses an effortOlder models (gpt-4.1, gpt-4o) don't reason; the tool retries without the effort and says so in the log
