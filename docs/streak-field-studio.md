# Streak Field Studio

Studio lives in Payload under **Assets > Studio Looks**. The plugin is registered in `src/plugins/index.ts`. It authors every effect in `features/immersive/studio/effects` (the Streak Field and the light leak); this page is written around the Streak Field, the workflow is the same for both, and [studio-effects.md](studio-effects.md) covers the contract, the light leak's slot controls and adding an effect.

## Editorial workflow

A Streak Field is used like a media file. A visual slot holds the field, the site shows what is published, and editing happens in Studio. There are no versions to pick and nothing to re-pin: publish the field and every place that uses it shows the result.

**From a page (the usual way).** In any visual slot (hero, block, tab, menu preview) choose Streak Field. The slot has one picker: the shipped looks, **New field**, and "Use a field made in Studio".

1. **New field** creates a field named after the page and the slot ("About · Hero"), starting from the shipped look the slot shows now, and opens Studio in a drawer at that slot's placement. The page stays open behind it.
2. Tune it, then **Publish**. The two posters render in your browser in a few seconds and the field is published with them in the same request. Close the drawer: the slot shows the poster and the title.
3. Later, **Edit field** opens the same drawer. Tune, Publish, done: the page updates by itself, with no second publish. **Change** goes back to the picker.
4. With a field of your own in the slot, seed, speed and intensity are hidden: those belong to Studio, where the poster is rendered from the same numbers. They still adjust a shipped look, which has no editor. Pointer and the poster override stay.
5. A page cannot be published while its slot holds a field that was never published (the slot says so). Until then the page shows the slot's shipped look.

**In Studio (Assets > Streak Fields).** A field has a **draft**, which autosaves, and what is **published**. The header says where the draft stands ("Published, no changes", "Draft, 4 changes since published") and where the field is used. **Reset to published** puts what is on the site back in the draft. The Published list and the History tab hold every published state, from Payload's own versions: clicking one shows it on the stage beside the draft (looking never changes anything), Restore copies it into the draft. Reset and Restore copy the recipe and the ground; the recipe is one undo step (the ground is not), and neither changes the site until you publish. Publishing or unpublishing a look busts every cached page, so each place that uses it shows the change on its next request.

- **Studio tab.** The stage shows the live field at a placement (hero, block, menu, card) on a dark or light face, with a budget line under it (particles, DPR, octaves, segments; amber when the placement cap bites). Starters load a shipped look into the draft. Draft and Compare switch the stage between the draft and a comparison (a published state, or a draft you chose to Keep); any edit, undo or redo returns the stage to the draft. Under the stage: the face toggle with the **Ground** control beside it, placement, playback, then the seed and capture frame of what the stage shows and **Variations**. Undo and redo hold fifty steps for the page (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z). Space pauses, R randomizes the seed, C toggles the comparison, Esc leaves a still, while the stage has focus.
- **Ground** is the look's own colour under the effect for the face shown: a swatch that opens the system colour picker, the hex, and a clear once it departs from the site background. It sits with the face toggle because it is a property of the face, not of the effect: it is painted on the stage, under a filled still, and on the site wherever the look sits in a frame of its own (cards, menu previews, block frames). It is a field of the look beside the recipe (`ground.dark`, `ground.light`), not a parameter: it saves with the draft, counts as a change to publish, and is not an undo step. The posters stay transparent; the frame paints the colour.
- **Variations** opens a contact sheet of what the stage shows: the same shot from other seeds (Arrangement, with Shuffle for another roll) and at other moments of the run (Moment, eight frames across the range), rendered in your browser at half the pixels. Choosing one sets the seed or frame at once; the stage behind renders the exact result. The moments come from one run read back at each frame, so a strip costs one simulation.
- **Inspector (sidebar).** Look, Pointer and Stills tabs. On the Look tab every section starts closed, on every document, so the panel opens as a contents page: each header carries the section's summary and a dot when something in it departs from the default. The double chevron at the right of the tab strip opens or closes them all. Pointer is a single group, so its rows are flat, with Reset beside the note above them. Every numeric parameter is a slider row: label, a track with an always-visible thumb, and a value field. The label's tooltip carries the parameter's meaning, range, step and default; a dot, blue fill and a tick at the default mark a changed value; option-click the label to reset it, or Reset a group from its header. A row that needs another setting (Flow speed under Drift) shows muted with the reason and a one-click fix. Min and max length share one track with two thumbs that cannot cross; only the fields can, and the server's message shows under the row.
- **Stills** (Inspector tab) are shots of the look for one use each, rendered in your browser and published with the look. The live look is drawn with content over it, so it can afford to be sparse; a still stands on its own, so it may be denser or brighter without touching what runs live. The list opens with **Poster** (the look exactly as it runs, both faces, with alpha; its seed and frame are the recipe's) and holds whatever you add: **Preview card** (1200 × 630, the share image of every page that opens on this look and sets no SEO image of its own; one per look), **Social post** (1080 × 1350), **Square post**, **Story** (1080 × 1920) and **Custom size** (320 to 1920 a side, WebP or JPEG, alpha optional). Choosing one puts it on the stage, rendered exactly, with its own seed, moment and face in the transport and Variations to find the shot; the Look and Pointer tabs then edit that still's own overrides (the dots mark what departs from the look, the tooltip's base reads "look", Reset on the Stills tab clears them), and the bar under the tab strip says so until Done. The Stills tab holds the output: use, name and size for a custom still, scale, format, alpha, and the ways out: **Open** (a new tab; it renders first if the site has no current render), **Download** and **Remove**. Each row says where it stands: on the site, re-renders on publish (its own settings, the look under it or the ground it is laid on moved), or not published. Up to twelve stills a look.
- **Publish** renders the two posters and every still that is not on the site as the draft has it, files each in Media, and publishes the look with all of them in one request; the button says what it is rendering as it goes. It is disabled while the draft is what is published, and says "Publish to 3 places" when the field is shared. To take a shared field in a different direction for one page, duplicate it and point that page at the copy.
- **Details tab** holds title, description, tags, the two posters, archive, authorship and **Used on** (documents whose draft uses the field are marked). Edits there never touch the recipe.

Archive hides a field from the picker; places that use it keep working. A field in use (by a document or its latest draft) cannot be deleted: remove it there first. The posters and the stills of a published field cannot be edited or deleted in Media; publish the field again to replace them.

**Restoring a page** works as it does for media. A page version stores the field's id, not its pixels, so a restored page shows the field as it is published today. The field's own history lives in Studio.

### Admin UI stack

The Studio components (`src/plugins/streak-studio/components`) are built from the site's shadcn primitives in `src/components/ui` (Slider, Tabs, ToggleGroup, Tooltip, Select, Input, Kbd) and Payload's own `Collapsible`, `Button`, `Link` and `toast`. Tailwind reaches the admin through one plugin-owned entry, `components/studio.css`: utilities only (no preflight), sourced from the primitives and the plugin, with the shadcn tokens painted from Payload's `--theme-*` scale so the Studio follows the admin's light and dark toggle. The token map, state variants and press recipe are shared with the site through `src/styles/shadcn-theme.css`. Nothing overrides Payload's own classes, every hook is a public `@payloadcms/ui` export, and components are registered by import-map path, so a Payload upgrade changes nothing here beyond what it changes for any custom field.

## Single source of truth

- `features/immersive/ui/streak-field-tuning.ts`: effect defaults.
- `features/immersive/studio/effects/streak-field.ts`: authorable parameters, the light face, the production envelope.
- `features/immersive/studio/recipe.ts`: the recipe engine every effect shares (validation, snapshot, identity, capture limits).
- `features/immersive/visual/placement.ts`: existing placement ceilings.
- `features/immersive/visual/posters.ts`: page and menu poster resolution.
- `plugins/streak-studio`: the looks collection, authorization, publication, slot hydration, and admin UI.

Drafts store versioned deltas, and the look's stills beside them (`stills`, a JSON list; `studio/stills.ts` is the contract: the uses and their sizes, `validateStills`, `stillTuning`, `stillKey`). Publish writes what the site renders from onto the look itself, the way image sizes ride on a media document: the resolved light and dark snapshot, the poster manifest, the two poster uploads, the hash of the published recipe, and each still's render and hash (`snapshot`, `posters`, `thumbnail`, `lightPoster`, `sourceHash`, `stills[].media`, `stills[].hash`, `stillMedia`; hidden or server-written, only by Publish). A draft write keeps a still's inputs and drops its outputs; the publish guard holds the live outputs through a Revert to published. A visual slot stores the look's id in `shader.studio`. `hydrateStudioFields` walks each document once, batches every nested slot per request, and replaces the id with the published look's `{ id, sourceHash, snapshot, posters, ground, stills }`, read from the published document and nothing else, since it lands in public page responses; `stills` carries only the uses the site serves (`SITE_STILL_USES`: the preview card), each read from its media row. `ground` is the look's own colour per face (`studio/ground.ts`), outside the snapshot and its hash: the slot's frame carries it as custom properties and globals.css ("Visual ground") paints the face the ground resolves to, so a theme flip is a style change and no poster is re-rendered. A field that was never published keeps its bare id and the slot falls back to its shipped look. Public pages receive a compact descriptor, never the library or editor, and render through the existing poster-first lazy adapter and its admission, pause, visibility, reduced-motion, capability, and fallback behavior.

The preview still is served by `documentShareImage` (`utilities/shareImage.ts`): a page's share image is its SEO image, else its hero's upload, else the preview still of the look grounding its hero, else Site Info's default. `generateMeta` and the JSON-LD builders read the same function, so the card a link unfurls to and the image search engines index are one choice.

Identity is the snapshot. `recipeHash` (`plugins/streak-studio/hash.ts`) hashes the resolved snapshot in canonical, key-sorted form, because Postgres `jsonb` returns keys reordered and the editor keeps the order it built; "draft matches published" in the stage uses the same canonical comparison, stills and ground included (`components/status.ts`). A still's identity is `stillKey`: the renderer, its resolved tuning (the recipe's deltas, its own on top, its seed, its face, the hero caps), its frame, its geometry and encoding, and the ground a filled one is laid on; the published still stores the SHA-256 (`stillHash`). A look can only be published with its posters and its stills' renders: the `beforeChange` guard accepts the Publish endpoint's write (a request-context pass, handed over for that one write and taken back) or output that is already published (Payload's Revert to published), and nothing else.

The initial authoring envelope fixes strokes at one segment and caps noise octaves, count, DPR, and high-overdraw combinations. Count is the one resource number the Inspector may set (Composition, 100 to 8000): it is what density reads as, and its range stops at the hero ceiling, so a recipe can ask for less than code allows and never for more. Limits apply after creative overrides. Studio uses the same effective limits. This conservative envelope is a policy, not proof of 60 fps on every GPU; use the existing performance measurement runbook for target-device qualification. Software Chromium capture is not a real-device benchmark.

## Rendering

Stills render in the editor's browser. `captureStills` (`features/immersive/studio/capture.tsx`) mounts the effect's own scene on its own offscreen canvas, advances fixed 1/60-second simulation steps with neutral pointer input, draws and encodes each frame asked for (one run serves a whole strip of moments), and takes the canvas down. Intermediate visible draws are skipped; flow simulation steps still run. Output scale changes pixel resolution without changing the logical layout; the contact sheet renders at half scale. Every capture goes through one queue (`captureSerial`): the still preview on the stage, the contact sheet and a publish render one at a time, and a capture overtaken by the next edit is aborted. The live preview pauses for the moment a publish takes, so the capture has the GPU to itself.

`POST /api/streak-looks/:id/stills` files one still: it takes the recipe, the still as the draft holds it and the image, checks both against the saved draft, verifies the image is at exactly the still's size, converts a PNG fallback and files it in Media. `POST /api/streak-looks/:id/publish` takes the recipe, the two posters and `renders` (still id to the media each render was filed as). In one transaction it locks the look, checks that the recipe it was sent is the saved draft (so the posters match the field), resolves every still to a render (the one sent, else the live look's when the still's hash is unchanged, else it refuses), verifies each poster is at exactly the poster size, files them in Media, and publishes the look with its snapshot, posters and stills. Both are team-only (`authenticated`, never `Boolean(req.user)`). Nothing renders or encodes inside a visitor page request, and there is no queue, worker, lease or Chromium function: the poster comes from the GPU the editor previewed on.

Pixels vary slightly between GPUs and drivers; the generated files, not a promise of identical pixels, are authoritative. Renderer compatibility changes require a version bump; a published field whose renderer the site no longer supports falls back to its preserved posters. After such a bump, republish each field from Studio (a one-off local Playwright script can do it for a long list).

## Database and rollout

`20260917_220809_streak_field_studio` added looks with native versions, plus the earlier release and render tables. `20260919_000344_streak_field_as_media` moves to the media model: it adds `studio` to every visual slot and version table and the published fields to looks, points every slot that pinned a release at that release's look, and gives each look what its newest release carried, so the site renders the same pixels from the look from the first request on. It is additive. Stills add a `stills` JSON column to looks and their versions and a `media` relationship (`stillMedia`) on the looks' rels tables, additive again; a look with no stills publishes as before. The `streak-releases` and `streak-renders` collections and the `release` column on every slot remain as bare, hidden schema that nothing reads; a follow-up migration drops them (pure removals, so `migrate:create` asks nothing). Local development uses Drizzle push; deployment CI applies the migrations. No migrations are applied to the local push-managed database.

Preview deployments run on their own Neon branch. The Neon deployment action on the Vercel store connection is enabled for `production` and `preview`, so each git preview deployment (the `preview` branch) gets a Neon branch named `preview/<git-branch>` forked from the production branch, and the branch connection variables are injected at deploy time (they are not stored in Vercel env settings). The build then runs `payload migrate` against that branch, so the production ledger only changes on `main` deploys. A deployment the webhook does not cover (a CLI `vercel deploy`) builds with production credentials, so `pnpm ci` runs `scripts/guard-preview-db.ts` first: a non-production build that sees the `PRODUCTION_DB_ENDPOINT` host, or no `POSTGRES_URL` at all, aborts before `payload migrate` instead of touching production, and a production build from any ref but `main` aborts too. The Vercel-managed Neon project is not visible to the local Neon CLI; manage branches from the Vercel Storage tab (Open in Neon) or with a Neon org API key.

Placement-specific poster generation remains outside this workflow. A slot's crop can differ from the field's poster; use a custom still through the existing poster override when exact art direction matters.
